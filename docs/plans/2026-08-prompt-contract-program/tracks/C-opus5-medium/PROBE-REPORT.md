### Two corrections to the plan's own wording

**`revision` is a state-transition counter, not a content counter.** Across every phase-B and phase-C tap the observed `(revision, state)` pairs top out at revision 2 — 1 while open, 2 on reaching a terminal state. A content change arrives as a **new `promptId`**, never as a bump. The row's intent (the card follows content changes and ignores cursor moves) holds; its stated mechanism does not.

**There is no "ghost" state on the wire.** A prompt goes `open -> resolved` with nothing between; `answering` is internal to the registry and emits no frame. The plan's "accept -> ghost -> resolved" described a *client-side optimistic* state as though the contract announced one.

## Phase D — v1.70.4, after #721's fix

**D10 holds, and this is the first time any of it was assessable.** On the multi-select form the card shows guidance with **zero tappable rows**, the composer issues no request (the guard is server-side: `/input` returns `409 prompt_pending`), and a **well-formed** direct `POST /prompt/answer` — correct promptId, revision, questionId, a real optionId — is refused **`400 unsupported_prompt_shape`**, non-destructively, leaving the prompt open at revision 1. Phase B accepted that identical request with `200 resolved`. Zero bytes on the answer path, with the transcript's only `tool_result` reading as a rejection with no option recorded.

**Two clarifications the row forced.** The card's Cancel *does* write to the PTY — it sends the provider's own ESC as a `keys` body, which reaches `sendKeys` ahead of the open-prompt check exactly as phase A recorded for #692. So "zero bytes" is a property of the **answer path**, not of the card as a whole, and the dismiss is the documented raw-key path rather than a hole. And the "Escape closes it" sub-check is **not verified**: the observed close was the 60-second expiry, 30 s after the keystroke.

**#730 (P2) — two producers, order-dependent, frozen.** A **transcript**-sourced prompt (`provenance: {source: "transcript", confidence: "authoritative"}`) carries the correct 2-question payload; a **screen**-sourced one (`source: "screen"`, `confidence: "inferred"`) carries a degraded 1-question scrape of the visible tab with two TUI affordances mistaken for options. Provenance predicted the parse on every observation of the two-question form (6 degraded / 5 correct across 11 prompts). Whichever producer lands last wins — the screen scraper is permitted to overwrite the authoritative transcript prompt, observed directly with `terminalReason: "replaced"` at ~5.89 s in both sightings — and because a payload is never revised in place, the winning parse is frozen for that promptId's life. **The fix needs no schema change**: `provenance.confidence` is already the discriminator. That is the second time this track found the contract carrying a distinction it does not act on; `provider_closed` was the first.

**#724's lifetime rule, measured rather than predicted.** One rule covers every observation: **a screen-sourced prompt's lifetime is its form's lifetime on screen; a transcript-sourced prompt expires at 60 s.** Screen-sourced, 7/7, ended when the form left the screen — six within 95 ms of a PTY write, lifetimes 1.2 s to 309.9 s, none on a timer. Transcript-sourced, 4/4 that were neither answered nor overwritten, expired at 60.001 / 60.002 / 60.010 / 59.949 s. So the durable phantom is a **consequence of the form staying up**, which is why it co-occurred with the degraded parse: a strictly untouched prompt took exactly one PTY write in 290.5 s — the ESC that ended it.

**No regression.** The single-select gate and single-select question signed off on 1.70.2 map and answer identically on 1.70.4, each verified by its provider-side effect rather than its status code: the command ran, the selection was recorded. One was screen-sourced and the other transcript-sourced, so **the answer path works from either producer** — #730 is a payload-completeness defect, not an answerability one.

## Findings

**#720 (P1) — outcome inference. FIXED in v1.70.2 (PR #726), verified.** `performAnswer` calls the adapter, which writes to the PTY, then re-checks the prompt's state. Answering a screen-scraped gate removes it from the screen, the detector marks the record `cancelled`, and the post-adapter check reports failure for a write that already succeeded. The per-entry queue serialises answers, so the re-check only ever sees detector-driven transitions — this cannot resolve the other way on a scraped provider. Three consequences: the false negative (row 2), the false positive (row 7's 200 on an unanswered form — the only 200 the route produced, differing from row 2 only by whether the prompt vanishes from the screen), and teardown minting a prompt that can outlive the answer indefinitely (3b) — split out as its own issue, threadbase-streamer **#724** (P1), which also carries the `provider_closed` discriminator below. Fix requirement recorded on the issue: a post-answer snapshot must hold zero teardown-minted open prompts. **#724 remains open, and phase C narrowed it to a three-tier result**: on 1.70.2 a permission gate's transient self-cancels in 0.0-0.4 s, a correctly-classified single-select question menu's lasts 60 s and then self-cancels, and only the misclassified #721 form leaves a **durable** actionable phantom. So the residual is specifically the misclassified-form case, not "the question-menu case" — a 60-second actionable window is not durable state, and it is not nothing: a client subscribing inside that minute receives an actionable phantom.

**#721 (P1) — screen→contract mapping. FIXED to its scope in v1.70.4 and closed; the residual is #730.** Multi-select detection and glyph-stripping are reliably repaired; what remained was traced to two producers rather than one scraper (see phase D). A multi-question, checkbox, Submit-bearing multi-select is reported as single-select with one question, the second dropped from the payload and checkbox glyphs leaking into labels as literal text. A fail-closed guarantee that depends on correct classification is not fail-closed when classification is wrong. Independent of #720 — each would remain wrong if the other were fixed, and they compound in row 7.

**#709 — legacy identity-free answer shape, two facets, closed by design on the contract path.** `optionIndex` is consumed as an array position while the frame hands clients 1-based `index` values, so an honest off-by-one returns 200 and executes a *more permissive* option (this occurred to the probe's own harness mid-phase and disabled gating for later rows); and `contentKey` is content-addressed, so it cannot separate two successive gates with identical content. 2b is the evidence that opaque `optionId`s remove the class rather than mitigate it.

**Unfiled, out of scope, still real — the streamer logs its API key unmasked at boot.** `"msg":"API key: tb_…"` sits on the same log line as the `apiKeyMasked` field. Once per boot on both versions (1x in the 1.70.0 log, 9x in the 1.69.6 log), so it predates the work under test; the source is `cli/index.ts:367`, where the message string interpolates the full key while the structured field beside it masks it. Filed as threadbase-streamer **#723** (P2, security). That key is the server's authentication boundary and the log is what people attach to bug reports — the 105-file scrubbed mirror exists because of this line as much as because of the harness's own argv.

**A terminal reason the broken path never consults.** A final snapshot recorded a prompt dismissed with Escape reaching `cancelled` with `terminalReason: "provider_closed"` — so this build has three observed terminal outcomes that discriminate the paths cleanly: provider-side dismissal gives `provider_closed`, an answered permission gate gives a bare `cancelled` (#720's false negative), and an answered question menu gives `resolved`/`answered` (#721's false positive). The registry can already tell "the provider closed this" from "this was answered"; it simply is not consulted where #720 breaks.

## The cross-version regression

The headline is neither defect alone. On identical detector behaviour, **the contract path is structurally more exposed to teardown transients than the path it replaces**: the legacy client keyed its card on gate *content*, which was identical across the transient, so it rode it without flicker; the contract client keys on `promptId`, which is new by construction, so it accepts the artefact as a new prompt. Legacy lifetimes 13/29/4/0 ms; contract 19/74/23/30 ms plus one that never terminated. In the durable case one on-screen question became two registry records — one `resolved/answered` that was not answered, and one `open` that nobody asked — and the `resolved` record is what any "did the user approve this?" audit would consult. The fix is the server's (do not mint a new open record on re-detecting the prompt just answered); the client keeps `promptId` keying, and the content-keying observation explains the exposure rather than proposing a return to it, which would reintroduce the #871 class. Whether the 19–74 ms frames paint is **unsettled** at a 250 ms sampling floor — the client is protected there by brevity, not by suppression.

## What the contract does deliver

Confirmed on a live wire, not discovered: `gateId` ≡ `promptId` (#700's occurrence-identity design); the **unconditional** empty snapshot, so "nothing pending" is asserted rather than inferred from silence; opaque option ids; `provenance {source: "screen", confidence: "inferred"}` being honest that a scraped gate is inferred; and cursor state being unrepresentable in the contract. Row 5(a) is a real observability gain: 1.69.6 could only show that *something* was replayed, while 1.70.0 hands the subscriber what is pending, with id, state and revision.

## Incidents and method

Two harness defects were found by controls rather than by luck, and both are in the close-outs: the probe's own answer script hit the #709 off-by-one and silently disabled gating (caught by a positive control with a never-approved command, remediated, and re-proven by a rule-present/rule-absent causal test), and a zero-bytes proof that returned zero for its control because grep cannot match through ANSI escapes (redone de-escaped). An isolation incident — 79 seconds across two boots sharing the user's real `cache.db` before the scratch `HOME` fix — is recorded in full, with `PRAGMA integrity_check` on a copy returning `ok` and the caveat that non-attributable writes in that window cannot be excluded. Standing rules the phase earned: prove provenance by a field the other shape lacks; an unscrolled screenshot is not evidence of absence; never run a check whose green result would mean the opposite (the same-key replay was declined for exactly this reason); rebuild a positive control when a defect removes the usual one.

## Verdict

Phase 2's exit criteria are **met for every prompt shape the app answers, and the one open defect is a payload-fidelity problem on a shape the app correctly refuses**. The legacy half was met thoroughly in phase A. The contract half failed on **v1.70.0** for reasons that were entirely server-side, held on **v1.70.2** for single-select permission gates and single-select questions after #720's fix, and on **v1.70.4** closes the last measurable gap: D10 holds — the multi-select form is refused with `400 unsupported_prompt_shape` and zero answer bytes, the app fails closed with a guidance card and a server-side composer block, and neither single-select path regressed. What remains open is **#730** (P2): two producers publish the same form, the lossy screen scrape may overwrite the authoritative transcript payload, and the winning parse is frozen for that prompt's life — a fidelity defect, not a safety one, and fixable without a schema change because `provenance.confidence` already names which payload to trust. **#724** stands explained rather than merely bounded: a screen-sourced prompt lives as long as its form is on screen, a transcript-sourced one expires at 60 s, so the durable phantom is a consequence of the form staying up. **The mobile client at 40ac02ac was correct in every row of all four phases, including every failure**; nothing found in this probe implicates the app or the contract's design. On that basis I would sign Phase 2 off against v1.70.4, with #730 tracked as a fidelity follow-up rather than a gate — and I would re-run the multi-question mapping row once #730 ships, since it is the only row whose result is a coin flip rather than a measurement.
